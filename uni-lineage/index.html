Two student histories, side by side.

No scores, no ranking, no winner. Both of them lost their mascot.

The columns are not equally deep. UCL is 83 years older, and had a history of its students’ union written up in 2023. Loughborough has no equivalent, so its column is shorter in places where the record is, not where the past was.

UCL 1826

Founded to admit the people Oxford and Cambridge would not. No colleges, then or now.

Loughborough 1909

Founded as a technical institute in the town centre. Halls on campus from 1932.

Founding

UCL

  • 1826Founded. Opens to students in 1828 as the self-styled London University.
  • 1828Admits nonconformists, Roman Catholics and Jews. Thomas Arnold calls it “that godless institution in Gower Street”.
  • 1832The Wilkins Building is finished. The money ran out in 1829 with only the portico and dome standing.
  • 1871The Slade School of Fine Art opens, teaching women on the same terms as men from its first day.
  • 1878Women admitted to degrees.
  • 1940The Blitz guts the library and brings down the dome.
  • 1954The main building is fully restored, rebuilt to Wilkins’s original specification.

Loughborough

  • 1909Founded as Loughborough Technical Institute, in the town centre.
  • 1915Herbert Schofield becomes Principal. He holds the post for 35 years and serves as president of the Students’ Association while running the college.
  • 1932Rutland Hall opens. The first students to live on campus.
  • 1937Hazlerigg Hall opens beside it, around the Fountain Lawn.
  • 1963Royce Hall opens, named after Sir Henry Royce of Rolls-Royce.
  • 1966University status.

The union

UCL

  • 1893Council approves the Union Society on 3 June, for “the promotion of social intercourse and of the means of recreation, physical and mental”. Men only. The first of its kind in the country.
  • 1897A Women’s Union Society is founded, because women were barred from the other one.
  • 1940sThe union runs free healthcare for students, before the NHS exists.
  • 1946The two unions merge in April, fifty-three years after the date on the plaques.
  • 1948Margaret Richards is appointed Permanent Secretary and serves 23 years. Her definition: “partly representation, partly a social focus for the students, and partly a trade union for the students”.
  • 1952Sheila Fitzgerald is elected President, the first woman to hold it and, as of the union’s own 2023 history, the only one.
  • 1975The role of Union President is abolished outright, replaced by three sabbatical officers of equal status.
  • 1979Occupation, over a campaign on sexual violence. Union publications advise hard-up students on squatting: “If you act collectively with others, you will usually be fairly secure”.
  • 1986A Women’s Officer is created. The following year, a large occupation demands a bigger union building.
  • 2014The Institute of Education merges in and UCL becomes majority postgraduate. The union then administered 230 clubs.
  • 2000sDeficit budgets and falling engagement leave the union, in its own account, “relegated to the peripheries of student life”.
  • 2018Sabbatical officers campaign for UCL to face its role in eugenics. That campaign produces the inquiry that reports in 2020.
  • nowOver 400 groups. Every one must publish a constitution and admit any student, which is why UCL cannot have a secret society.

Loughborough

  • 1975LSU takes its current form on 1 August, serving the university and two colleges.
  • Six sections: welfare and diversity, societies, media, enterprise and employability, rag, action.
  • The Athletic Union sits alongside them, representing over 60 student-led clubs.
  • 2019The Executive settles at five officers, elected every February and March, scrutinised by a Union Affairs Committee, a Student Forum and a Board of Trustees.
  • One officer, Student Communities, holds action, rag, societies and student voice together.
  • Each hall elects its own committee: a chair, social reps, sport reps. Halls collect points in Intra-Mural Sport all year, weighted by each sport’s grading.

The clubs

UCL

  • 1828A Medical Society forms almost immediately. Medics dominated the first cohorts.
  • 1829A Debating Society, founded as the Literary and Philosophical Society. Three years after the university itself.
  • 1858A Reading Room Society, formed to give students a sense of community thirty-five years before there was a union.
  • 1897The union buys a sports ground at Acton: eighteen grass courts, two cricket squares, a running track. The Boat Club still descends from it.
  • 1900sMusic, Debate and Drama are funded centrally and free to join. Film Soc and, per the union’s history, the Jazz Society are added later.
  • 1906The first student play: The Importance of Being Earnest.
  • 1900sA Critical Society, founded “for the consideration of philosophical, social, and religious questions without any restrictions as to standpoint”.
  • 1948Film Society founded. It owns a Steenbeck editing suite and 16mm cameras, which almost no university did.
  • 1989Christopher Nolan picks UCL for that equipment. He meets Emma Thomas in his first week and brings her into the society.
  • 1990sThey run 35mm screenings across the year and spend the takings on 16mm film in the summer. Larceny 1996, shot over a weekend on the society’s kit. Doodlebug 1997, scored by David Julyan, another member. Following 1998, on about $6,000.
  • 1996Four students meet in Ramsay Hall and form the band that becomes Coldplay.
  • nowThe Institute of Making is free to every student: laser cutters, 3D printers, woodwork, ceramics and a Materials Library.

Loughborough

  • 1919Students of Loughborough College form the original rugby side in September.
  • 1920A football club follows, founded as Loughborough College.
  • 1930The Loughborough Colleges XV enters the Universities Athletic Union and beats Nottingham 8–0 in its first match. The club has since won the national championship twenty-seven times.
  • The Motor Club is reportedly the oldest club on campus. Members get a workshop, a full tool set, a hydraulic press and a car trailer.
  • Weekly garage sessions, all levels, from an oil change to an engine rebuild.
  • Elsewhere on the list: hot air ballooning, a Computer Society, and a joint Scout and Guide club.
  • 1963Royce Hall opens and builds its own calendar. St Fred, its patron saint, gets a day a year with inflatables, punch and a barbecue.
  • 2010Peter Holmes, Royce’s rag rep, runs 100 laps of the hall for charity. He dies that year in an accident near Vancouver.
  • nowRoyce runs those 100 laps every year in his memory. It is the hall’s largest rag event.
  • Action, the volunteering section, puts over 800 students into local projects.

The press

UCL

  • 1830The London University Chronicle. It lasts a few editions.
  • 1937Phineas, a union newspaper named after the mascot. Evacuated out of London during the war, as was the statue.
  • 1946Pi, named after the Provost, Sir David Pye. It launches as deliberately non-political, in a college running everything from a Conservative Society to a Revolutionary Socialist Society.
  • The standard of the Refectory is a permanent grievance across all of it.
  • 2004The Cheese Grater, investigative and satirical, founded in February. Its 2018 investigation into UCL and race is cited in the union’s own official history.
  • 2011The Graters, its sketch group, form and take a show to the Edinburgh Fringe every year since, apart from 2020.
  • nowPi runs online, in print and as PiTV, eighty years on. When UCL commissioned a history of its own union, Pi was the source it was written from.

Loughborough

  • 1970sLCR starts broadcasting. It is the oldest section of LSU Media.
  • 1980sThe Alternative Alternative Show begins, and has run continuously since. The longest-running student radio show in the UK.
  • 1997Label, the student magazine, out of a predecessor called The Newspaper.
  • 2001LSUTV, expanded out of the Media Centre. Six awards at the student television awards in 2020, including best documentary.
  • 2014Lens, the photography section, founded under its first head, Isabella Piggott.
  • Four sections under one masthead: Label, LCR, Lens and LSUTV.

Rag

UCL

  • 1900sRag runs as stunts, races and hijinks, usually pointed at King’s.
  • 1927A rag magazine is published to mark UCL’s centenary.
  • 1950sBonfires are lit in the Main Quad every year until this decade.
  • 1960sStudents challenge the charity framing and ask for real community work instead.
  • nowThe union’s own history re-evaluates rag for its “darker, sexist undertones”.

Loughborough

  • 1920sA rag parade starts running through the town, and has ever since.
  • by 1972The rag magazine is called Thor, after the mascot. Both universities named a student publication after a statue.
  • 1979Records begin. Rag has raised over £18 million since.
  • 1984A hitchhike to Paris.
  • 2015£1,393,847.08 in one year, the sixth in a row above a million.
  • 2019Named student fundraising group of the year.
  • nowThe largest student fundraising organisation in Europe. Raids are bucket collections in other cities, usually with a night’s stay so collectors catch the morning rush hour.
  • Bigger asks on the list: Kilimanjaro, a skydive, cycling the coast of Scotland. The committee includes Alumni Officers, whose job is the line back through previous years.

Mascots

UCL

  • 1832Jeremy Bentham dies on 6 June and leaves instructions. He is publicly dissected by Southwood Smith, then preserved, dressed in his own clothes and seated in a cabinet.
  • 1850UCL takes the auto-icon. The head mummified badly, so a wax one sits on top of it.
  • 1900Students steal a wooden Highlander from a shop on Tottenham Court Road during a parade for the relief of Ladysmith. He becomes Phineas.
  • 1932The store gives Phineas to UCL. He becomes the official mascot, and later meets the King and Queen.
  • 1975King’s students steal Bentham’s head and demand £100 for Shelter. UCL pays £10 and gets it back.
  • 2002The real head goes into a climate-controlled store at the Institute of Archaeology.
  • 2019The union votes to retire Phineas over the Boer War parade he came from.
  • 2020The auto-icon moves to the Student Centre, where it still sits. The story that he is minuted “present but not voting” at council meetings is a myth, and UCL says so itself.

Loughborough

  • 1958Students of Hazlerigg-Rutland weld Thor in the college welding shop: an oversized knight’s helmet with the visor down, copied from the helmet in the old college crest.
  • 1980sThor is on display in the Students’ Union foyer.
  • sinceMissing. The union has made a film about him. The university has said publicly that nobody is really sure where the real Thor is.

The bar

UCL

  • 1947The first Union Bar opens in the South Junction. One barman, Charlie, whose stories amused “even water drinkers”. Beer and cigarettes, no spirits.
  • 1947It is immediately judged “the most popular aspect of Union activities” and “the real common denominator of student life”.
  • 1950sThe Garage, a hut on the bombsite where the Great Hall stood, hosts the Saturday night hop. One student called it “where you went to find a boyfriend”.
  • 1959The union moves into 25 Gordon Street, a former hospital the Provost called “very far from adequate” before anyone moved in. It is still there.
  • 1981Cocktails night launches. Queues run around the block, in a building licensed for 6.5% of the student body.

Loughborough

  • 30+ yrsFND, the Friday night, has run for over thirty years in the union’s own building. Described as the biggest student club night in the UK.
  • Hey Ewe runs on Wednesdays, to celebrate that day’s sporting results.

Enterprise

UCL

  • The Hatchery, a two-year incubator at BaseKX in King’s Cross, for students and graduates within two years.
  • Your company must already be registered at Companies House before you can apply.
  • Eight weeks of induction to start: one workshop on a Thursday evening plus two online sessions, every week.

Loughborough

  • LSU Enterprise is a section of the union itself. No company required to walk in.
  • Then the Start-Up Lab, which bridges the union’s hub and The Studio, and then LUinc. out on the science park.
  • The whole ladder is called the Loughborough Enterprise Network, and is meant to run from a student’s first idea to a first institutional round.
  • The graduate start-up programme carries up to £7,500.

Also on the record

UCL denamed the Galton Lecture Theatre, the Pearson Building and the Pearson Lecture Theatre on 19 June 2020, following an inquiry into its history with eugenics, and issued a formal apology on 7 January 2021.

UCL’s bicentenary runs through 2026. Anyone starting in 2027 misses it by a year.

Questions for the visits

The only part of this you cannot check from a desk.

Ask at UCL

  1. Stand in the Octagon under the dome, then go and look at Bentham. Twenty minutes answers the atmosphere question.
  2. Does the Film & TV Society still hold its own equipment, and who runs the festival?
  3. Where do first years actually end up living, and does anyone stay near campus after that?

Ask at Loughborough

  1. What happens in halls if you do not play Intra-Mural Sport?
  2. Has anyone found Thor?
  3. How does a rag raid actually get organised, and who pays for the travel?
